# 01 — Quant Researcher Career Map

> What the role actually looks like at each stage, who hires, and what the progression is.

---

## 1. What a QR does day-to-day

Research work splits into several tracks depending on the firm type:

**Alpha research (equity / futures / crypto):** Generating and testing predictive signals — factors that explain future returns. This is the systematic equivalent of "stock picking": find something that works, prove it works statistically, run it live, and watch it decay. The statistical discipline required to not fool yourself here is enormous — most signals that look good in-sample are noise.

**Derivatives pricing and model development:** Building models to price and hedge complex instruments (exotic options, structured products, CVA/XVA). More applied maths, less pure ML. Common at banks and derivatives-heavy hedge funds.

**Risk and portfolio construction:** Building risk models (factor models like Barra/Axioma), VaR, expected shortfall, stress tests. Translating statistical models into risk limits and portfolio constraints.

**ML / alternative data research:** Applying ML to financial prediction problems — NLP on earnings calls, computer vision on satellite imagery, graph models on transaction networks. The frontier, and where "your stated interest in AI/ML and interpretability" lands most directly.

---

## 2. Firm types and what each looks like

**Quantitative hedge funds** (AQR, Two Sigma, D.E. Shaw, Man AHL, Renaissance, Winton): The primary QR employer. Research-first culture — your job is to find alpha. Large research libraries, proprietary datasets, significant compute. Interview processes are among the most rigorous anywhere, including PhD-level statistics questions.

**Stat-arb / multi-strategy hedge funds** (Millennium, Citadel hedge fund, Point72): More pod-based; researchers typically work within a pod with a PM. Closer to the P&L. Often faster feedback loops between research and live trading.

**Investment banks (quantitative strategies / equity derivatives research):** Derivatives modelling, risk model development, exotics pricing. More structured, more process-heavy, less pure alpha research.

**Asset managers with quant teams** (BlackRock Systematic, Vanguard Quant, PIMCO quant): Larger AUM, longer time horizons, more factor-model focused. Less raw alpha, more portfolio engineering.

**Central banks / regulators:** Risk models, economic research, macro forecasting. Lower comp, more stability, more policy influence. A valid path if the academic/research side appeals more than the trading side.

---

## 3. Career progression

```
Year 0–2 (junior researcher):   Running experiments defined by seniors.
                                  Learning the research infrastructure and process.
                                  Owning one small signal or model component.

Year 2–5 (researcher):           Generating and testing your own signal ideas.
                                  Owning a research area end-to-end.
                                  Presenting results to PMs and seniors.

Year 5–10 (senior researcher):   Leading a research track.
                                  Mentoring juniors.
                                  More direct input on portfolio decisions.

Year 10+ (principal / PM):        Either managing a pod as a PM
                                  or becoming a research director.
                                  At this stage, P&L ownership is often part of the role.
```

The QR path has the longest ramp — you need 2–3 years of rigorous statistical practice before you're reliably not fooling yourself. Patience here is not optional.

---

## 4. Education expectations

QR roles at top firms increasingly expect or prefer PhDs — particularly in mathematics, statistics, physics, CS, or financial engineering. This is not a hard requirement everywhere, but the realistic picture is:

- **Top HF QR roles (AQR, Two Sigma, D.E. Shaw):** ~50–70% of researchers have PhDs. Strong MSc + exceptional track record competes.
- **Mid-tier HF / bank QR:** BSc/MSc with strong research output (a thesis, a published paper, or demonstrable original work) is realistic.
- **Your BBS Financial Engineering:** Is a solid foundation. The distinguishing factor will be the research output you build during and after it — the QUANTFRAME concept deep-dives, a strong FYP, and the projects in this folder.

The honest advice: a master's (MSc Quantitative Finance, MSc Financial Mathematics, MSc Statistics) from a recognised programme significantly widens the door. Target Warwick, UCL, Imperial, Edinburgh, Carnegie Mellon, LSE, or comparable. The vault you're building right now is exactly the right preparation for those applications.
