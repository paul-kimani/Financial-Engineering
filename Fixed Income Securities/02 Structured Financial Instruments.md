These slides dive into the mechanics of **Structured Financial Instruments**, explaining how derivatives are embedded into fixed-income securities to alter their risk-return profiles, and detailing how to calculate the coupon structure for an **Inverse Floater**.

## 1. Types of Structured Financial Instruments

Structured notes typically modify traditional cash flows by combining a standard debt security with an embedded derivative option or swap.

- **Core Structure:** These instruments combine a traditional bond framework with a derivative instrument to give holders customizable exposure to one or more underlying asset classes, such as equities, bonds, or commodities.

- **Capital Protected Instruments:** A classic example is a **guarantee certificate**, which packages a _zero-coupon bond_ (to guarantee the return of principal at maturity) together with a _long call option_ (to capture upside potential from the underlying asset).

- **Participation Instruments:** These track the performance of an underlying metric without leverage. A common example is a standard **floating-rate bond**, where the coupon adjusts directly alongside a market reference rate.

- **Leveraged Instruments:** Designed to magnify returns and offer high potential payoffs from small initial outlays. A prime example is an **inverse floater**.


## 2. Inverse Floaters (Leveraged Notes)

An **Inverse Floating Rate Note (IFL)** is a leveraged instrument where the cash flows are adjusted periodically but move in the **opposite direction** of the market reference rate. When reference rates fall, the coupon payment on an inverse floater rises, making it highly sensitive to interest rate shifts.

### The General Formula

The coupon rate for an inverse floater is mathematically expressed as:

$$\text{IFL Coupon Rate} = C - (L \times R)$$

- $C$ = The maximum coupon rate achieved if the market reference rate ($R$) falls to exactly zero.

- $L$ = The coupon leverage factor.

- $R$ = The underlying Market Reference Rate (MRR).


## 3. Step-by-Step Example Solution

The final slide presents a structured finance problem where an 8% fixed-rate collateral pool is sliced into a standard floater and an inverse floater.

### Given Data:

- **Total Collateral Pool:** $3,000,000 par value paying an 8% fixed coupon.
- **Asset A (Floater):** Par value of $2,000,000 paying $\text{MRR} + 1\%$.
- **Asset B (Inverse Floater):** Par value of $\$1,000,000$ paying an unknown structured rate ($?$).

### Solution:

**Step 1: Calculate the total annual coupon cash flow generated by the underlying collateral.**

$$\text{Total Coupon Available} = 3,000,000 \times 8\% = 240,000$$

**Step 2: Express the total annual cash flow obligated to Asset A (Floater).**

$$\text{Cash Flow to A} = 2,000,000 \times (\text{MRR} + 0.01) = 2,000,000 \times \text{MRR} + 20,000$$

**Step 3: Determine the residual cash flow remaining for Asset B (Inverse Floater).**

Since the total cash flows generated by the structure must balance out:

$$\text{Cash Flow to B} = \text{Total Coupon Available} - \text{Cash Flow to A}$$

$$\text{Cash Flow to B} = 240,000 - (2,000,000 \times \text{MRR} + 20,000)$$

$$\text{Cash Flow to B} = 220,000 - 2,000,000 \times \text{MRR}$$

**Step 4: Convert the residual cash flow into a percentage coupon rate for Asset B.**

Divide the cash flow by Asset B's specific par value ($1,000,000):

$$\text{Coupon Rate}_B = \frac{220,000 - 2,000,000 \times \text{MRR}}{1,000,000}$$

$$\text{Coupon Rate}_B = 0.22 - (2 \times \text{MRR})$$

### Final Answer:

The explicit coupon structure for the Inverse Floater (Asset B) is:

$$\mathbf{22\% - (2 \times \text{MRR})}$$

- Here, the maximum possible coupon ($C$) is **22%**.

- The coupon leverage factor ($L$) is **2**, meaning for every 1% increase in the reference rate, the inverse floater's coupon drops by 2%.
