This list outlines a **structured framework for taking notes on academic sources** (like journal articles, books, or papers). It is designed to help you deconstruct what an author is saying, extract the essential evidence, and connect it to your own research.

### 1. The Big Picture
- **What it means:** The broad context, real-world problem, or overarching academic debate the paper addresses.
- **Question to ask:** _Why does this research matter in the grand scheme of things?_

### 2. The Big Point

- **What it means:** The central argument or core takeaway (thesis statement) of the work.
- **Question to ask:** _If I had to summarize the author’s main conclusion in one or two sentences, what would it be?_
### 3. Premise / Hypothesis

- **What it means:** The foundational assumptions or testable predictions the study starts with.
- **Question to ask:** _What does the author assume to be true, or what specific relationship are they trying to test/prove?_
### 4. Data

- **What it means:** The evidence, dataset, sample, or material collected to investigate the question.
- **Question to ask:** _What information did they look at (e.g., 500 survey responses, historical archives, interview transcripts, census data)?_
### 5. Theories / Conceptual Approaches

- **What it means:** The theoretical frameworks, lenses, or paradigms used to interpret the findings.
- **Question to ask:** _What established models or theories (e.g., Critical Race Theory, Cognitive Load Theory, Game Theory) are guiding their analysis?
### 6. Analytical / Research Methods

- **What it means:** The methodology and techniques used to process the data.
- **Question to ask:** _How did they run the study (e.g., qualitative thematic analysis, regression analysis, randomized controlled trial)?_
### 7. Results

- **What it means:** The direct findings and factual outcomes of the analysis.
- **Question to ask:** _What did the data actually show before the author added their broader interpretation?_
### 8. Quotations – Exact Words

- **What it means:** Powerful, perfectly phrased sentences taken verbatim from the text.
- **Tip:** Always include the exact page number here so you avoid accidental plagiarism later.
### 9. Citable Notes

- **What it means:** Key ideas summarized or paraphrased in your own words, ready to be cited in your own writing.
- **Tip:** Translating the author’s ideas into your own voice proves comprehension and speeds up draft writing.
### 10. Influence on Your Thinking

- **What it means:** Your personal critique, synthesis, and reflections.
- **Question to ask:** _How does this change my perspective? Does it support, challenge, or create a gap in my own project?_
