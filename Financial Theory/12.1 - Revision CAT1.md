## Question One

**Explain the concept of the certainty equivalent and risk premium. How do they relate to an individual's utility function? (4 marks)**

- **Certainty Equivalent (CE):** This is the **guaranteed amount** of wealth or cash that an individual would accept instead of taking a gamble with uncertain outcomes. It provides the exact same level of utility as the expected utility of the risky gamble.

- **Risk Premium (RP):** This is the difference between the expected value (actuarial value) of a gamble and its certainty equivalent. It represents the **minimum amount of expected return** an individual demands as compensation for taking on the risk.

- **Relation to Utility Function:**

    - **Risk Averse:** The utility function is concave ($U''(w) < 0$). The certainty equivalent is less than the expected value ($CE < EV$), resulting in a positive risk premium ($RP > 0$).

    - **Risk Neutral:** The utility function is linear ($U''(w) = 0$). The certainty equivalent equals the expected value ($CE = EV$), resulting in a risk premium of zero ($RP = 0$).

    - **Risk Seeking:** The utility function is convex ($U''(w) > 0$). The certainty equivalent is greater than the expected value ($CE > EV$), resulting in a negative risk premium ($RP < 0$).

## Question Two

**a) Describe in words any four axioms of Expected Utility Theorem. (8 marks)**

1. **Completeness:** An individual has well-defined preferences and can always compare any two alternatives. For any choices A and B, the individual either prefers A to B, prefers B to A, or is completely indifferent between the two.

2. **Transitivity:** Preferences are logically consistent. If an individual prefers option A to option B, and prefers option B to option C, they must necessarily prefer option A to option C.

3. **Continuity:** If an individual prefers A to B, and B to C, there exists some probability $p$ (where $0 < p < 1$) such that they are completely indifferent between receiving B with certainty and participating in a gamble that offers A with probability $p$ and C with probability $(1 - p)$.

4. **Independence:** If an individual prefers option A to option B, introducing a third, irrelevant option C will not change their preference. A gamble between A and C will always be preferred to a gamble between B and C, assuming the probability of receiving C is the same in both scenarios.

**b) State the conditions for an investor to be non-satiated and risk neutral in terms of their utility function, $U(w)$. (4 marks)**

- **Non-satiated:** The investor always prefers more wealth to less. Mathematically, the first derivative of the utility function with respect to wealth must be strictly positive:

    $$U'(w) > 0$$

- **Risk Neutral:** The investor's marginal utility of wealth is constant, meaning they only care about expected value and not variance. Mathematically, the second derivative of the utility function with respect to wealth must be zero:

    $$U''(w) = 0$$

**c) Outline the consumer choice theory. (10 marks)**

Consumer choice theory is an economic framework that models how individuals make consumption decisions based on their preferences and budget constraints to maximize their satisfaction (utility). The core components are:

- **Preferences and Utility:** Consumers have distinct preferences for different bundles of goods. These preferences are mapped to a utility function, which assigns a numerical value to the satisfaction gained. This is often represented graphically using indifference curves, where any point on a single curve yields the same level of utility.

- **Budget Constraint:** Consumers face limited income, restricting what they can afford. The budget line represents all possible combinations of goods a consumer can purchase if they spend their entire income, determined by the prices of the goods.

- **Utility Maximization:** The rational consumer seeks the highest possible utility given their budget. This optimal choice occurs at the point where the budget line is perfectly tangent to the highest attainable indifference curve. At this point, the marginal rate of substitution (the rate at which the consumer is willing to trade one good for another) exactly equals the ratio of the prices of the two goods.

- **Underlying Assumptions:** The theory assumes that consumers act rationally, possess complete information, their preferences are transitive and complete, and they adhere to the principle of non-satiation (more is always better than less).

## Question Three

**A consumer is offered a gamble with a 60% chance of receiving $120 and a 40% chance of receiving $80. Assume a utility function $u(w) = w^{0.5}$.**

Let $w_1 = 120$, $p_1 = 0.6$

Let $w_2 = 80$, $p_2 = 0.4$

**i) Calculate the actuarial value of the gamble. (2 marks)**

The actuarial value is the Expected Value ($EV$):

$$EV = p_1w_1 + p_2w_2$$

$$EV = (0.6 \times 120) + (0.4 \times 80)$$

$$EV = 72 + 32$$

**EV = $104**

**ii) Compute the expected utility of the gamble. (3 marks)**

Expected Utility ($EU$) is calculated using the utility function $u(w) = w^{0.5}$:

$$EU = p_1u(w_1) + p_2u(w_2)$$

$$EU = 0.6(120^{0.5}) + 0.4(80^{0.5})$$

$$EU = 0.6(10.954) + 0.4(8.944)$$

$$EU = 6.572 + 3.578$$

**EU = 10.15**

**iii) Determine the certainty equivalent. (2 marks)**

The Certainty Equivalent ($CE$) is the guaranteed amount that gives the same utility as the expected utility of the gamble:

$$u(CE) = EU$$

$$CE^{0.5} = 10.15$$

$$CE = (10.15)^2$$

**CE = $103.02** _(or $103.03 depending on rounding at the EU stage)_

**iv) Calculate the cost of the gamble. (1 mark)**

The cost of the gamble is the Risk Premium ($RP$), which is the Expected Value minus the Certainty Equivalent:

$$RP = EV - CE$$

$$RP = 104 - 103.02$$

**Cost of the gamble (RP) = $0.98**

**v) Is the consumer risk averse, risk neutral, or risk seeking? (2 marks)**

The consumer is **risk averse**.

This is proven because the Certainty Equivalent ($103.02) is strictly less than the Expected Value ($104). Alternatively, it can be proven by checking the second derivative of their utility function, which is negative ($u''(w) = -0.25w^{-1.5} < 0$), indicating a concave utility function characteristic of risk aversion.
