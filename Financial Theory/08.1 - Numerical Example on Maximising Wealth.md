# 08.1 — Numerical Example on Maximising Wealth

> A worked application of [[08 - Fisher's Separation Theorem]]. Given a firm's
> Investment Opportunity Set and the Capital Market Line, we locate the optimal
> investment at the tangency point and read off the interest rate ($25\%$),
> average and marginal returns, PV/NPV, and total firm value ($\$8.0\text{m}$) —
> confirming that the firm's total present value is just the $x$-intercept of the CML.

---

## 1. How much should the company invest in the project?

The optimal investment amount is found at the point of tangency between the firm's curved Investment Opportunity Set and the straight Capital Market Line (CML).

- **Initial Cash:** The firm starts at the X-intercept of the curve, which is **$5.2m**.
- **Optimal Retained Cash:** Dropping a vertical line from the tangency point to the X-axis shows the firm should retain **$3.2m**.
- **Investment Amount:** The firm invests the difference.

    **$5.2m - $3.2m = $2.0m**

---

## 2. What is the interest rate?

The market interest rate ($r$) is derived from the slope of the Capital Market Line (CML). The CML passes through the Y-intercept at $(0, 10)$ and the X-intercept at $(8, 0)$.

$$ \text{Slope} = \frac{\Delta Y}{\Delta X} = \frac{0 - 10}{8 - 0} = -1.25 $$

Because the slope of the CML is equal to $-(1 + r)$:

$$ -(1 + r) = -1.25 \implies r = 0.25 $$

The interest rate is **25%**.

---

## 3. What is the average rate of return on the investment in plant?

The average rate of return is the total profit generated by the project divided by the initial cost. At the optimal investment point, the $2.0m investment generates a Year 1 payoff of **$6.0m** (the Y-coordinate at the tangency point).

$$ \text{Average Return} = \frac{\text{Total Payoff} - \text{Initial Investment}}{\text{Initial Investment}} $$

$$ \text{Average Return} = \frac{6.0 - 2.0}{2.0} = 2.0 $$

The average rate of return is **200%**.

---

## 4. What is the marginal rate of return?

A core rule of the Fisher Separation Theorem is that a firm should continue investing in physical assets until the marginal rate of return exactly equals the market interest rate. At the tangency point, these rates are equated. Therefore, the marginal rate of return is **25%**.

---

## 5. What is the present value and NPV of the investment?

First, calculate the Present Value (PV) of the expected Year 1 payoff by discounting it at the 25% interest rate:

$$ \text{PV} = \frac{\text{Future Value}}{1 + r} = \frac{6.0}{1.25} = 4.8\text{m} $$

Next, calculate the Net Present Value (NPV) by subtracting the initial cost of the investment:

$$ \text{NPV} = \text{PV} - \text{Initial Investment} = 4.8 - 2.0 = 2.8\text{m} $$

The PV is **$4.8m** and the NPV is **$2.8m**.

---

## 6. What is the total present value of the company?

The total present value (or total wealth) of the company is its initial cash reserves plus the wealth created by the investment (NPV).

**$5.2m + $2.8m = $8.0m**

_Note: You can easily verify this on the graph. The total present value of the firm is simply the X-intercept of the Capital Market Line, which sits perfectly at **8**._

---

## 7. The One Rule Doing All the Work

Every answer above is an instance of a single principle from [[08 - Fisher's Separation Theorem|Fisher separation]]: **invest until the marginal rate of return equals the market interest rate.** At that tangency point:

- the **marginal** return has fallen to $r = 25\%$ (Q4), even though the **average** return is still $200\%$ (Q3) — the gap between them is the surplus that becomes NPV;
- the resulting $\text{NPV} = \$2.8\text{m}$ (Q5) is *created wealth*, and total firm value $= $ initial cash $+$ NPV $= \$8.0\text{m}$ (Q6), which is exactly the CML's $x$-intercept.

Separation means this optimal *production* decision is independent of the owner's *consumption* preferences — the reason the same discount rate $r$ and NPV rule reappear everywhere, from real investment ([[06 - Real Investment Decision]]) to the discounting of derivative payoffs in [[../Stochastics/14 - Asset Dynamics under the probability measures|the Stochastics notes]].
