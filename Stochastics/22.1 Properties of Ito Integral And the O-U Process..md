---

## 1. The Definition and Properties of the Itô Integral

This page introduces the mathematical setup of the **Itô Integral** and its fundamental expectation and variance properties.

- **Definition of the Itô Integral:**

    When a function $f(t)$ is integrated with respect to a standard Brownian motion $W_t$, we write it as:

    $$I(t) = \int_0^t f(s) dW_s$$

    This is not a Riemann-Stieltjes integral because the paths of a Brownian motion are of infinite variation. It requires Itô's specific stochastic framework to define properly.

- **Property (a) — The Mean (Martingale Property):**

    Because a standard Brownian motion has an expected change of zero at any point, the expectation of any Itô integral is always zero:

    $$E[I(t)] = E\left[ \int_0^t f(s) dW_s \right] = 0$$

- **Property (b) — The Variance (Itô Isometry Intro):**

    We can first start by outlining the variance derivation. Since the mean is $0$, the variance of $I(t)$ is equal to the expectation of its square:

    $$\operatorname{Var}(I(t)) = E\left[ \left( \int_0^t f(s) dW_s \right)^2 \right]$$

    The math at the bottom of the page sets up how we calculate this by analyzing the variance of the infinitesimal Brownian increments, $d\operatorname{Var}(W_s) = ds$.

---

## 2. Itô Isometry & Solving $\int_0^t W_s dW_s$

We complete the variance property of the Itô integral and walk through a classic calculus example.

- **Itô Isometry:**

    The top formula states a fundamental theorem in stochastic calculus—**Itô Isometry**:

    $$E\left[ \left( \int_0^t f(s) dW_s \right)^2 \right] = \operatorname{Var}\left( \int_0^t f(s) dW_s \right) = \int_0^t f(s)^2 ds$$

    This is extremely powerful because it allows us to compute the variance of a complex _stochastic_ integral by evaluating a simple _deterministic_ time integral!

- **Solving $\int_0^t W_s dW_s$:**

    In standard calculus, $\int x dx = \frac{1}{2}x^2$. However, in stochastic calculus, we must account for the quadratic variation of the Brownian motion.

    To solve this, we guess a function $f(s, W_s) = \frac{W_s^2}{2}$ and apply Itô's Lemma:

    - $\frac{\partial f}{\partial s} = 0$

    - $\frac{\partial f}{\partial W_s} = W_s$

    - $\frac{\partial^2 f}{\partial W_s^2} = 1$

    Plugging these into Itô's Lemma:

    $$d\left( \frac{W_s^2}{2} \right) = 0 \cdot ds + W_s dW_s + \frac{1}{2} (1) ds = W_s dW_s + \frac{1}{2} ds$$

    Integrating both sides from $0$ to $t$:

    $$\frac{W_t^2}{2} = \int_0^t W_s dW_s + \int_0^t \frac{1}{2} ds \implies \frac{W_t^2}{2} = \int_0^t W_s dW_s + \frac{1}{2}t$$

    Isolating our target integral yields the famous result:

    $$\int_0^t W_s dW_s = \frac{1}{2}(W_t^2 - t)$$

---

## 3. Starting the General Ornstein-Uhlenbeck (O-U) Process

This page introduces the SDE for the O-U process (commonly used to model interest rates and mean-reverting currency pairs) and starts the derivation to find its analytical solution.

- **The O-U SDE:**

    $$dX_t = -\theta X_t dt + \sigma dW_t$$

    _Where $\theta$ is the rate of mean reversion, and $\sigma$ is the volatility._

- **The Integrating Factor Trick:**

    To solve this SDE, we define a helper process $U_t$ using an integrating factor $e^{\theta t}$ (see [[22.2 Helper Process]] and [[22.3 Decoupling]]):

    $$U_t = X_t e^{\theta t} = f(t, X_t)$$

- **Applying Itô's Lemma to $U_t$:**

    We calculate the partial derivatives of $f(t, X_t) = X_t e^{\theta t}$:

    - $\frac{\partial f}{\partial t} = \theta X_t e^{\theta t}$

    - $\frac{\partial f}{\partial X_t} = e^{\theta t}$

    - $\frac{\partial^2 f}{\partial X_t^2} = 0$

    Substituting these and the SDE for $dX_t$ into the Itô expansion:

    $$dU_t = \theta X_t e^{\theta t} dt + e^{\theta t} dX_t$$

    $$dU_t = \theta X_t e^{\theta t} dt + e^{\theta t} (-\theta X_t dt + \sigma dW_t)$$

    $$dU_t = \theta X_t e^{\theta t} dt - \theta e^{\theta t} X_t dt + \sigma e^{\theta t} dW_t$$

---

## 4. Finalizing the O-U Process Solution

This page finishes the algebraic cancellation and solves for $X_t$.

- **The Cancellation:**

    The two drift terms above cancel out perfectly ($\theta X_t e^{\theta t} dt - \theta X_t e^{\theta t} dt = 0$), simplifying our helper process differential to:

    $$dU_t = \sigma e^{\theta t} dW_t$$

- **Integrating Both Sides:**

    Integrating from $0$ to $t$:

    $$\int_0^t dU_s = \int_0^t \sigma e^{\theta s} dW_s$$

    $$U_t - U_0 = \int_0^t \sigma e^{\theta s} dW_s$$

- **Substituting $U_t = X_t e^{\theta t}$ back in:**

    At $t=0$, $U_0 = X_0 e^0 = X_0$. Therefore:

    $$X_t e^{\theta t} - X_0 = \int_0^t \sigma e^{\theta s} dW_s$$

    $$X_t e^{\theta t} = X_0 + \int_0^t \sigma e^{\theta s} dW_s$$

- **Isolating $X_t$ (The Final Solution):**

    Divide both sides by $e^{\theta t}$ (which is the same as multiplying by $e^{-\theta t}$):

    $$X_t = X_0 e^{-\theta t} + e^{-\theta t} \int_0^t \sigma e^{\theta s} dW_s$$

    By moving the deterministic $e^{-\theta t}$ factor inside the integral (since it doesn't depend on the integration variable $s$), we get the final general solution of the O-U process:

    $$\mathbf{X_t = X_0 e^{-\theta t} + \int_0^t \sigma e^{-\theta(t-s)} dW_s}$$
